Case studies / Aristocrat Technologies

Cutting design-to-development time 35% on a casino management platform

Aristocrat's casino management software runs cash, player and compliance workflows on live casino floors. I built the UX team that is moving it from legacy WinForms to Angular, and set the design system and accessibility rules every product team now ships against.

Context

A platform casinos can't switch off, built on WinForms

Casino operators use the platform to manage player accounts, cage and cash, floor operations and regulatory reporting. The legacy products were built on WinForms and are being modernised to Angular. how many products, and how far their patterns had drifted apart

What I owned: UX strategy, hiring and structure of the UX team, design system governance, accessibility standards, and the AI design-to-code initiative. What the team shipped: screen design, UX writing and component code across product lines.

Stakes & problem

Every inconsistent screen was paid for twice: once in build time, once in operator error

Business problem. Modernising to Angular product by product would multiply the inconsistency unless the patterns were fixed first. evidence of duplication, e.g. the same table rebuilt in N products

User problem. Floor and back-office staff work under time pressure with money and regulated data. Inconsistent patterns mean slower tasks and higher error risk.

Cost of doing nothing. Quantify: e.g. duplicated components per product, average handoff cycle in days, or audit findings

Research

What discovery showed about the 35%

how you found where design-to-dev time was going: handoff audit, engineer interviews, ticket analysis

  • Finding 1: e.g. most rework came from spec gaps, not design changes

    Decision it changed: we put a design-engineer inside the UX team so specs shipped as Angular components, not static files.

  • Finding 2: e.g. accessibility defects were found late, in QA

    Decision it changed: WCAG 2.1 AA rules moved into component tokens and states, so every product inherits them.

Architecture & iteration

A governed system, then AI to close the last gap

  • Call 1: Fix the patterns before migrating the screens

    I chose to build the shared component and token library before teams ported modules to Angular. What you said no to, e.g. a pixel-for-pixel port of WinForms screens, and why

  • Call 2: Governance with a named owner and a review ritual

    Every new pattern goes through a design system review before it reaches a product team. Describe the ritual: cadence, who attends, how exceptions are approved

  • Call 3: Pilot Claude AI for design-to-code

    Once components and tokens were stable, I started an initiative using Claude to generate Angular scaffolding from design specs, with the design-engineer reviewing output.

Iteration. One thing you tried that didn't work and what changed. E.g. first AI output ignored tokens; you constrained prompts to the component library and quality jumped.

Alignment. How you brought product directors, engineering architects and compliance along. One concrete example.

Solution

One pattern library across products, with accessibility enforced in code

Accessibility proof

confirm each item below matches what shipped

  • Contrast

    All text and state tokens meet 4.5:1; large text and icons meet 3:1.

  • Keyboard

    Every component has a documented tab order and visible focus ring.

  • States

    Error, warning and success never rely on colour alone.

  • Labels

    Form fields carry programmatic labels and error descriptions.

Impact

35% faster from design to development, and a team other products now borrow
