# Executive UX Portfolio

Help the user present their work the way hiring managers for senior design leadership read it: fast, outcome-first, and focused on decisions rather than deliverables.

## Who reads this portfolio

Assume the reader is a CPO, VP Product, VP Design or an executive recruiter. They skim for 60–90 seconds per case study. They want to know:

1. What was the business problem and what was at stake?
2. What did *you* decide, and what did you change about how the team worked?
3. What happened as a result, in numbers where possible?

Craft (wireframes, components, pixel polish) is assumed at this level. Show it as evidence, not as the story.

## Case study structure

Use this order. Each section should be short enough to read in under 30 seconds.

1. **Headline outcome.** One line with the result, e.g. "Cut claims processing time 42% across 3,000 adjusters."
2. **Context.** Company, product, team size, your role and reporting line, timeline.
3. **The problem.** Business problem first, user problem second. Name the cost of doing nothing.
4. **Your call.** The two or three decisions that shaped the work. Include what you said no to and why.
5. **How you led.** Team structure, stakeholder alignment, process changes, hiring, rituals you introduced.
6. **The work.** A curated handful of visuals that prove the decisions. Caption every image with what it shows and why it matters.
7. **Impact.** Metrics (revenue, retention, task time, NPS, support volume, adoption), plus org-level change (design maturity, team growth, new practices that stuck).
8. **Reflection.** What you'd do differently. One paragraph, honest.

## Writing rules

- Lead with the outcome, then explain how you got there.
- Use "I" for decisions you made and "we" for work the team shipped. Be precise about which is which.
- Prefer concrete numbers and names over adjectives. "Reduced onboarding from 14 steps to 5" beats "streamlined onboarding."
- If a metric is confidential, use relative figures ("2.3×", "~40%") or directional signals, and say so.
- Cut process theatre: no generic double-diamond diagrams, sticky-note photos or persona templates unless they changed a decision.
- Keep each case study under roughly 1,200 words.
- Avoid filler like "passionate", "user-centric", "seamless", "leverage", "robust", "holistic".

## Portfolio-level guidance

- Three to four case studies is enough. Pick ones that show range: a 0→1 product, a turnaround or redesign at scale, and one on building or scaling a team or design system.
- The homepage should state, in one or two sentences, what kind of leader the user is and what problems they're hired to solve.
- The about page should cover leadership philosophy, team sizes managed, domains, and a short career arc. Skip the list of tools.

## When reviewing an existing case study

Score it against the structure above and return:

1. The single biggest problem (usually: buried outcome, unclear personal role, or no metrics).
2. A rewritten headline and opening paragraph.
3. A short list of specific cuts and additions, quoting the lines to change.

Don't rewrite the whole thing unless asked.
